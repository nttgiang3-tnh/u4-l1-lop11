GRAMMAR

💡 Grammar — Gerunds: V-ing as Subject and Object

Gerund as SUBJECT 🌟

V-ing can stand at the beginning of a sentence as the subject. It counts as singular, so the main verb takes -s.

• Joining ASEAN activities helps students grow.

• Learning English opens doors for ASEAN youth.

Gerund as OBJECT after verbs 🎯

After verbs like enjoy, practise, suggest, finish, mind, use V-ing (not the infinitive).

• I enjoy learning about other cultures.

• She suggested holding a flag quiz.

🔗 Gerund after PREPOSITIONS — be interested in · look forward to · be good at · thank somebody for + V-ing:
• Nam is good at remembering flags. · We look forward to meeting students from Laos.
⚠️ In “look forward to”, the word to is a preposition — so use V-ing, NOT “to + verb”!

STAGE 3 · READING

📖 Reading — The ASEAN Youth Forum

Read the story, then answer the questions.

Green Star High School held an ASEAN Youth Forum on ASEAN Day, and Linh and Nam joined it with hundreds of students. In the school yard, there were ten colourful stalls, one for each member country of the association. Visiting the stalls in one morning was like travelling around Southeast Asia. At the Vietnamese stall, Linh put on a beautiful ao dai. “Wearing traditional costumes helps us understand each other's culture,” she said.

Near the stage, their teacher pointed at the big ASEAN emblem with ten rice stalks and explained that the rice is a symbol of the ten countries working together. She also reminded them of the ASEAN motto: “One Vision, One Identity, One Community”. After the speeches, students from different schools sang the ASEAN song together. Nam loved this part of the day because singing together helped the young people feel like one community.

The best moment came when the teacher opened a gold envelope and announced that Linh had won a scholarship to join a student exchange programme in Singapore. In the afternoon, Linh and Nam played a flag quiz game and learned to recognise the flags of all ten countries. At sunset, everyone took a group photo in front of the flags. “Learning about ASEAN makes me proud,” Nam said. “Our region is one big family.”

STAGE 3 · SPEAKING

🗣️ Speaking — Introduce an ASEAN country

Work in pairs. Choose an ASEAN country and introduce it to your partner: its capital, a traditional costume, famous food and one interesting custom. Then swap!

Useful language: I'd like to introduce… · Its capital is… · It is famous for… · Wearing… is a tradition there. · Travelling to… is exciting. · I'm interested in learning about… · I look forward to visiting it one day.

A: Which ASEAN country would you like to introduce?
B: I'd like to introduce Thailand. Its capital is Bangkok.
A: What is it famous for?
B: It is famous for its temples and delicious food. Wearing traditional costumes at festivals is a beautiful tradition there.
A: Would you like to visit it?
B: Yes! I'm looking forward to travelling there one day.

STAGE 3 · WRITING

✍️ Writing — My ASEAN Day

Write a paragraph about ASEAN Day at your school (70–80 words). Use gerunds as subjects and objects.

Sentence starters: Joining the ASEAN Youth Forum was… · At the forum, I enjoyed… · Wearing… helped me… · I am interested in… · Learning about ASEAN makes me feel…

Joining the ASEAN Youth Forum at my school was an unforgettable experience. In the morning, I visited ten stalls and tried delicious food from different countries. I enjoyed wearing a traditional costume and singing the ASEAN song with my friends. Playing the flag quiz helped me remember all ten flags. Learning about ASEAN cultures makes me proud of our region. I look forward to joining an exchange programme one day.
